Salt Lake, Joshua Tree, and Red Rock November 2, 2026

After spending the fall ramping up on my freshly re-assembled labrum, I was excited check out some new crags out West (first climbing trip in >18 months!). Claire and I planned for 3 days in Salt Lake before the office retreat in Joshua Tree, then another 3 days with Alexander and Charles in Red Rock Canyon.

Salt Lake City

A 90 minute delay on our Thursday night flight fortuitously ended with us getting to the car rental 10 minutes before they closed at 1:50 am. We made the short drive to Claire’s cousins and passed out after a quick shower.

Day 1: Maple Canyon & Fifth Water

We had an ambitious schedule planned for Friday:

  • grab coffee and snacks
  • 2 hour drive to Maple Canyon
  • The Dizzy Channel1
  • ~1 hour drive to the trailhead
  • 2.5 mi hike to Fifth Water Hot Springs
  • soak in the sulfur
  • hike back before sunset, head to dinner with the cousins

Odds of us rising early enough to complete this mission looked slim. Luckily Tuna (pictured below with her brother Fergus) woke us up when, at 8am, she headbutted our bedroom door.

I was a little nervous for The Dizzy Channel; it was Claire’s first real multipitch and my first time as the more experienced climber. Claire’s blossomed into a confident 5.9 leader and we’d done Clip a Dee Doo Dah a week earlier to practice the rope management but this was a big step up from a 5.3 scramble.

Finding the base of the climb took longer (30 mins? an hour?) than we’d hoped. If I stared at the rock for too long I began to question the strength of the sediment matrix that held the river cobbles together, but after a tentative first pitch we settled in and started to enjoy the climbing and views. Claire cruised her pitches and I almost numbed out on the crux. Despite the skepticism of Google AI Overview we actually caught the canyon towards the end of peak foliage.

I’d chosen The Dizzy Channel over Tachycardia, a classic in the area, to avoid rappels (exchanging it for a mild scramble down) but the descent ended up being spicier than expected due to recent rain. This was likely exacerbated by our questionable route-finding. Towards the end I broke a hold during a short downclimb2.

While we made quick time on the ascent, our meandering approach and descent cost us 2 hours of sunlight. Fifth Water is known to get busy during nights and weekends so we decided to skip food and beeline for the springs. Vibes during the hike in were therefore bad. They did pick up when we got to the springs in time for sunset, with just a few other people. 50-odd people passed us on the walk back.

Day 2+3: Big+Little Cottonwood Canyon

The Dizzy Channel was our main objective for the trip, and with that ticked we were happy to get a few short days in at the Cottonwoods. My shoulder didn’t feel quite ready to start yarding on 12s again so I decided to focus efforts on getting more comfortable onsighting moderate pitches.

After our long 1st day we opted for crags with short approaches and relatively scenic views (S-Curve in BCC and Melting Mud Wall in LCC). I made two important errors here:

  1. Assuming that the MP approach estimates3 would apply to 2 gumbies who exclusively climb at the most developed crag in the Northeast.
  2. Planning based on the conditions in SLC basin (sunny 55°) instead of the canyons (35 and snowing in LCC)

We didn’t last very long at either crag but I did enjoy trying the quartzite and limestone; personal favorites included Urban Sprawl and Get a Life at S-Curve and Antidote at MMW.
